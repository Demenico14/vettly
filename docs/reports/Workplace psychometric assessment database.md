# Ground Vettly's mock assessment catalogue in evidence

Workplace selection uses a stable set of about eighteen assessment families. They fall into three groups. Maximum-performance ability tests have right answers and run about 10–25 items in 10–25 minutes. Typical-performance self-report questionnaires (personality, values, trait emotional intelligence) are untimed and run 60–230 items. Hybrid job-specific measures include SJTs, integrity tests, work samples, typing and language tests. A realistic mock database has to model all three groups, along with the norm-referenced scoring chain that turns a raw score into a percentile, sten or band. The validity numbers Vettly shows have changed. Sackett et al. (2022) cut most meta-analytic estimates by .10–.20: cognitive ability falls from **.51 to .31**, and structured interviews (.42) and job knowledge tests (.40) now rank first. Mock "predictive power" figures should use the 2022 values, with Schmidt and Hunter (1998) shown only as history. For Southern Africa, the compliance picture is clearer than it was. **The HPCSA certification requirement in South Africa's EEA s.8(d) was struck down in 2017 and deleted with effect from 1 January 2025**, so no "HPCSA-certified" badge should exist. What still binds employers is evidence of validity, reliability, fairness and lack of bias; practitioner gating for HPCSA-*classified* tests; human review of automated decisions (POPIA s.71, Zimbabwe CDPA s.25, Zambia DPA); and Zimbabwe's POTRAZ licensing under SI 155 of 2024. On licensing, IPIP items are public domain and safe for commercial use. ICAR and HEXACO-PI-R are not, and every major vendor's product and scale names should be kept out of the mock data. Throughout this report, tables carry a **Basis** marker: **C** means the figure is cited, **A** means it is a design assumption, and **C/A** means a cited anchor extended by assumption.

## Eighteen test families sort into three measurement logics

Ability tests are built from a small number of item families: series, matrices, verbal and spatial. These combine into a general-ability ("g") composite. The open-source ICAR-60 shows the standard construction: **9 letter-and-number series, 11 matrix reasoning, 16 verbal reasoning and 24 three-dimensional rotation items**, validated on about 97,000 participants ([Condon & Revelle 2014](https://personality-project.org/revelle/publications/condon.icar.14.pdf)). One occupational vendor says **2–4 aptitude tests** are enough for an adequately g-loaded aggregate ([Test Partnership](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/test-partnership-assessments-theory.html)).

The commercial benchmarks are short. SHL's interactive numerical test is reported at **18 minutes and a maximum of 10 questions**. Its adaptive numerical test is reported at a maximum of 16 questions. Both figures come via search summaries, because the PDFs could not be parsed ([SHL factsheet](https://www.shl.com/assets/documents/rebranded-assets/product-factsheet-verify-interactive-numerical-reasoning.pdf); [SHL Numerical Ability factsheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/Verify_Numerical_Ability_Fact_Sheet.pdf)). The verbal format is dominated by True / False / Cannot Say judgements on a passage ([practiceaptitudetests](https://practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/verbal-reasoning-test-practice-true-false-cannot-say)). A leading critical-thinking appraisal runs **40 items in 30–40 minutes across five subtests**: inference, assumptions, deduction, interpretation and evaluating arguments ([TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/recruitment/assessments/watson-glaser-critical-thinking-appraisal.html)). The classic mechanical comprehension test runs **68 items in 30 minutes**, or 55 items in 25 minutes in its second edition ([Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_aptitude)). Checking tests are explicitly speeded; one sample runs 40 items in 15 minutes ([AssessmentDay](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/resources/attention-to-detail-test.html)).

Personality instruments follow either the Five-Factor or HEXACO structure, or a broader occupational "work styles" model. **The HEXACO-PI-R has 6 factors × 4 facets plus an interstitial Altruism scale**, in 200-, 100- and 60-item forms ([hexaco.org](https://hexaco.org/history)). The best-known occupational questionnaire has **32 scales in three domains**: relationships with people, thinking style, and feelings and emotions. Its forced-choice version uses **104 blocks scored by IRT**, and its normative Likert version has about 230 items ([SHL OPQ fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf); [Brown & Bartram](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/id/document/3179616)).

Emotional intelligence splits into two models with different formats. The ability model uses **141 items across 8 tasks in 4 branches in 30–45 minutes**, scored against consensus or expert keys ([EI Consortium MSCEIT](https://eiconsortium.org/measures/msceit.html)). The trait model is self-report: **153 items, 15 facets and 4 factors in about 25 minutes** ([EI Consortium TEIQue](https://eiconsortium.org/measures/teique.html)).

Integrity tests are either overt (direct attitude and admission items) or personality-based ([US OPM](https://opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/integrityhonesty-tests)). SJTs use most/least, rate, rank or single-best formats ([practiceaptitudetests](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/situational-judgement-test-response-formats/)). Their response instructions are either "would do" (behavioural tendency) or "should do" (knowledge) ([McDaniel, Psotka & Legree 2009](https://mcdaniel97.github.io/Publications/McDaniel,%20Psotka%20&%20Legree%20(2009,%20SIOP).pdf)). Typing tests report gross and net WPM on a 5-character word, over **3–5 minutes** with about 95% accuracy expected. Published benchmarks range from 35–55 WPM to 50–60 WPM ([EmployTest](https://employtest.com/typing-test-for-employment/); [Canditech](https://www.canditech.io/blog/what-is-a-good-typing-speed/)).

Delivery mode and item format are separate design choices, and the mock data should store them independently. Computer-adaptive testing (CAT) adjusts difficulty to each answer. This produces shorter tests and gives each candidate a different form, which improves security ([Test Partnership](https://testpartnership.com/adaptive-tests.html)). The cost is a large item bank. Banks hold **"hundreds of questions"**, and each new item is trialled on **250–500 participants** before it is IRT-calibrated ([Test Partnership](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/test-partnership-assessments-theory.html)).

On the personality side, forced-choice blocks resist faking. Classical scoring of those blocks yields ipsative data, which supports only within-person comparison ([Brown & Maydeu-Olivares](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4923072)). Thurstonian IRT and MUPP models recover approximately normative scores ([Frontiers in Psychology 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00806/pdf)). The validity advantage of IRT scoring over classical scoring has been inconsistent ([Fisher et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/234763786.pdf)). IRT scoring also does not fully remove ipsativity unless some blocks are unbalanced ([Missouri State 2026](https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/articles00/36)). In the mock data, a forced-choice questionnaire should therefore carry a scoring-model field (`classical_ipsative` or `thurstonian_irt`). Only the IRT variant should be allowed to produce between-candidate rankings.

## Every score is a raw score read through one norm group

The scoring chain is mechanical once a norm group is fixed. The conventions are z (mean 0, SD 1); **T = 50 + 10z** ([arXiv psychometrics guidelines](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09959)); sten from 1 to 10 (mean 5.5, SD 2, each band ½ SD wide), which is the UK occupational standard for personality ([Test Partnership: sten](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/what-is-a-sten-score.html)); and stanine from 1 to 9, with cumulative cut-offs at **4, 11, 23, 40, 60, 77, 89 and 96%** ([Test Partnership: stanine](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/what-is-a-stanine-score.html)). Expected sten band populations run 2.28, 4.41, 9.18, 14.99 and 19.15% from each tail inward ([Wikipedia: Sten scores](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sten_scores)). Mock score distributions can be checked against those proportions.

Vendors do not agree on 5-band cut-offs. Some use 10/20/40/20/10 percentile splits and others use 10/30/40/20, so the banding above is a design convention and not a standard. Report types are better documented. One vendor produces a recruiter ability report and a candidate version, a personal feedback report with **up to five development areas**, and an interviewer report "with suggestions for interview questions" that flags impression-management patterns ([Clevry reports](https://help.clevry.com/module-4-clevrys-reports); [Clevry interviewer report](https://www.clevry.com/en/resources/sample-reports/interviewer-report/)). Another platform separates a threshold-based "selection report" from a development report ([Testlify](https://help.testlify.com/article/699-types-of-reports-on-testlify)).

The mock catalogue therefore needs five report templates. The **HR report** shows the score table, the norm used, a composite, fit, and consistency flags. The **candidate feedback report** shows bands without percentiles, strengths, and at most five development areas. The **interview guide** covers 3–8 flagged scales, each with a hypothesis, probes and indicators. The **development report** and the **team report** complete the set. The section structures are design assumptions modelled on the cited examples.

Norm groups are where South African realism matters most. SHL's January 2024 norm list includes a **"Numerical Reasoning Manager Composite (South Africa)"** ([SHL support](https://support.shl.com/documents/987/attachments/5516)). Clevry publishes South African norm data for **2021–23**, which suggests a norm window of two to three years ([Clevry SA technical data](https://help.clevry.com/south-african-technical-data)). JvR publishes SA supplements to international manuals ([JvR MAS supplement](https://manuals.jvrpsychometrics.co.za/assets/MAS-SA-supplement.pdf)). One small vendor creates a language-specific norm only where **n ≥ 300** ([JobCannon](https://jobcannon.io/research/technical-manuals/aptitude-test)), and international pooled samples can reach about 28,000 ([Saville technical summary](https://www.savilleassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Analysis-Aptitude-Range-Technical-Summary.pdf)). No public Zimbabwe, Zambia or Botswana norms were found. Regional norms in the mock data are therefore fictional constructs and should be labelled as such.

## Validity estimates fell by .10–.20 once Sackett corrected the corrections

Reliability is the less contested half of the evidence. A meta-analysis of 1,359 Big Five alphas found means of **.73–.78** and test–retest stability of **.69–.76** ([Viswesvaran & Ones 2000](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/measurement-error-in-big-five-factors-personality-assessment-reli/)). Integrity tests averaged **alpha .81** (overt .82, personality-based .77) and **retest .68** in a 1993 report ([DTIC ADA254681](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA254681.pdf)). SJTs are the weak point. One meta-analysis of 56 alphas found **.46** ([Catano et al., via Goldsmiths](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/20393/1/IJT_Jan_17.docx)). A reliability generalisation study across 271 SJTs is cited secondarily at about .68 and described as below high-stakes standards ([Kasten & Freund 2016](https://fis.leuphana.de/en/publications/a-meta-analytical-multilevel-reliability-generalization-of-situat/); [arXiv citing .68](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.12144)). A short contemporary reasoning battery (32 items) reports **alpha .877** ([JobCannon](https://jobcannon.io/research/technical-manuals/shl-practice-test)). For planning, Sackett et al. assume ability-test reliability of **.80** ([Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)).

Validity is where the field moved. Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens (2022, *JAP* 107(11), 2040–2068) showed that earlier meta-analyses applied range-restriction corrections that were often inappropriate. Their re-estimates fall **.10–.20 below** the earlier figures. The mean of the top five predictors drops from **.49 to .37**, and job-specific "samples" (structured interviews, job knowledge, biodata, work samples) now lead ([Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf); [SMU repository](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894)). Even under extreme assumptions, cognitive ability validity reaches only about .50. At plausible assumptions it is about **.34**. A 2024 follow-up repeats that GMA validity was "considerably overestimated" ([Berry et al. 2024](https://x-mol.com/paper/1826118985217286144)).

The defensible position is to use Sackett as the headline and show Schmidt and Hunter as historical context. Those who prefer the older figures argue that Sackett under-corrects where indirect range restriction is severe. The 2022 sensitivity analysis answers this directly: even its most generous scenario leaves GMA below the 1998 value.

All values in the table above are from [Sackett et al. 2022, Table 3](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf) (Basis C). The d values come mainly from Dahlke and Sackett (2017), and some come from non-applicant samples.

Two older meta-analyses fill in sub-types. For counterproductive behaviour other than theft, integrity validity was **.39 for overt and .29 for personality-based tests** across 665 coefficients ([Ones et al. 1993, via DTIC](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA254681.pdf)). SJTs add **1–2% variance** beyond ability and the Big Five ([McDaniel et al. 2007, via review](https://typeset.io/pdf/situational-judgment-tests-a-review-of-practice-and-28k2pes4mu.pdf)). Composite figures of .76–.78 (GMA plus integrity, or GMA plus structured interview) rest on the corrections Sackett criticises ([Stafforini summary](https://stafforini.com/works/schmidt-2016-validity-and-utility/)). A mock battery validity of about **.40–.50** is the realistic ceiling (Basis A).

The subgroup-difference column matters more in Southern Africa than in the US. The top five predictors include three with large d (work samples, job knowledge, cognitive) and three with small d (structured interviews, biodata, integrity). Combining them reduces group differences ([Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)). South African evidence suggests the cognitive gap is entangled with language and schooling. On language- and education-sensitive measures, English-speaking, advantaged groups score highest, and local norms often fail to reflect linguistic diversity ([Stellenbosch thesis 2011](https://scholar.sun.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/ed98c8d4-48f7-4b79-801b-e3e232c6a252/content)). WAIS-III factor structures differ between SA and UK samples ([Cockcroft et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00297/full)). Nonverbal items show differential item functioning across five SA language groups ([SAJIP DIF study](https://sajip.co.za/index.php/sajip/article/view/881/1101)). Race and language differences in social desirability among 1,640 SA applicants were not explained by cognitive ability alone ([Odendaal 2015](https://doaj.org/article/6f424450ed2f4f0f884d027ccea27751)). No applicant-sample d estimates exist for Zimbabwe, Zambia or Botswana. Any mock adverse-impact data for those countries is invented and should be labelled that way.

Role-to-battery templates follow from this evidence, but public sources confirm only level-based packaging, such as Managerial & Professional and Supervisory & Team Leader versions ([Eras aptitude tests](https://feeds.jgp.co.uk/public_data/VacancyDocuments/0074/4151/Eras_aptitude_tests.pdf)). The one cited principle is that job knowledge tests and work samples suit trained applicants, while cognitive tests suit untrained applicants who will learn on the job ([Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)). The weighting follows Sackett's recommendation to make the structured interview the focal predictor. A suggested default is structured interview 35–40%, ability 25–30%, work sample or job knowledge 15–20%, and personality or SJT 10–15%. The CV screen should act only as a minimum-requirements gate, because years of experience predict at just **.07** ([Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)). The weights themselves are Basis A.

## South Africa dropped HPCSA certification on 1 January 2025

Section 8 of the Employment Equity Act now prohibits psychological testing "and other similar assessments" of an employee unless the test **(a) has been scientifically shown to be valid and reliable, (b) can be applied fairly to all employees, and (c) is not biased against any employee or group**. Paragraph (d), the certification requirement, was deleted by the Employment Equity Amendment Act 4 of 2022 with effect from **1 January 2025** ([EEA s.8, acts.co.za](https://source.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act-1998/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php)). The High Court had already set aside its commencement in *ATP-SA v President of the RSA* in May 2017 ([SAFLII](https://www1.saflii.org/za/cases/ZAGPPHC/2017/144.html); [SA Labour News](https://salabournews.co.za/37376-court-cancels-proviso-on-hpcsa-certification-of-psychological-tests-for-jobs)). The legislative memorandum noted that the HPCSA lacks the capacity to certify tests ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=836433)). One law-firm update refers to a "2019" ruling, which conflicts with the 2017 citation; the 2017 date is better supported ([Webber Wentzel](https://www.webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/psychological-testing-and-other-similar-assessments-of-employees-an-update.aspx)).

HPCSA classification is a different matter and still applies. The Psychometrics Committee keeps a list of tests classified as psychological (Form 207). The copy found dates from about 2010–11 ([UFS copy of Form 207](https://www.ufs.ac.za/docs/librariesprovider25/cpd-documents/cpd-psychological-tests-(form207)-1011-eng.pdf?sfvrsn=0)). Classified tests may be used only by HPCSA-registered practitioners working within the scopes set by GN R.993 of 2008 ([HPCSA GNR 993](https://www.hpcsa.co.za/Uploads/professional_boards/psb/regulations/regulations_gnr993_2008.pdf)). Psychometrists may administer, score and give a preliminary interpretation. An HPCSA FAQ says psychometrists and registered counsellors may not use certain tests, including personality and neuropsychological instruments ([HPCSA FAQ, UFS copy](https://www.ufs.ac.za/docs/librariesprovider25/cpd-documents/cpd-psychology-frequent-asked-questions-1015-eng.pdf)). For Vettly, classification determines *who may use* a test, and s.8 evidence determines *whether it may be used at all*.

POPIA adds the automated-decision constraint. Under s.71, a candidate may not be subjected to a decision with legal or substantial effect that rests solely on automated profiling of "performance at work", reliability or conduct. The exceptions require appropriate measures, including a chance to make representations and enough information about the logic of the processing. The employer remains the responsible party even when a vendor processes the data ([Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/when-machines-make-decisions-understanding-the-impact-of-the-protection-of-personal-information-act-2013-popia/)). Other POPIA mappings in the table below (ss.11, 14, 18, 22, 26–33, 72) come from background knowledge and are marked unverified. They should be checked against the [official Act](https://www.justice.gov.za/inforeg/docs/InfoRegSA-POPIA-act2013-004.pdf).

Zimbabwe has the heaviest administrative load of the four countries. The Cyber and Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07] took effect on 11 March 2022, with POTRAZ as the authority ([Veritas CDPA](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf)). Section 25 gives a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions, unless the data subject consents or a law provides for it ([Veritas CDPA](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf)). Sensitive data needs written consent ([Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/zimbabwes-cyber-and-data-protection-act-overview/78795)).

**SI 155 of 2024, in force from 13 September 2024, requires a POTRAZ licence for any controller processing data of 50 or more people.** It also requires a DPO notified within 90 days, breach notice to POTRAZ within **24 hours**, and notice to data subjects within 72 hours where the risk is high ([Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/understanding-si-155-of-2024-new-regulations-on-data-protection-licensing-and-data-protection-officers-in-zimbabwe)). Licence fees reportedly run from US$50 to US$2,500 a year ([CITE](https://cite.org.zw/potraz-data-licence-fees-slammed-as-costly-burden-on-businesses-and-consumers/)). Only licensed persons may process biometrics ([Business Times](https://businesstimes.co.zw/understanding-the-purpose-scope-of-the-cyber-and-data-protection-licensing-regulations-in-zimbabwe/)). POTRAZ announced compliance inspections from 1 September 2026 ([Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/potraz-urges-compliance-with-data-protection-laws/)). Sources conflict on the licensing deadline, giving 3 months, 6 months or March 2025 ([DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/zimbabwe/insights/2024/A-Quick-Start-Guide-to-Zimbabwes-Data-Protection-Regulations)). The Labour Act s.5 protects prospective employees from discrimination ([Chambers 2025](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/employment-2025/813)). Advice that only AHPCZ-registered psychologists should administer tests is expert opinion, not statute ([Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/engage-registered-psychologists-for-pre-employment-tests-expert/)).

Zambia's Data Protection Act No. 3 of 2021 requires controllers to register with the Data Protection Commissioner. It also requires sensitive data to stay in Zambia, or to be transferred only with consent, and gives a right to object to solely automated decisions ([DataProtection.Africa](https://dataprotection.africa/zambia/); [Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/zambia-employers-obligations-under-the-data-protection-act/)). The Employment Code Act 2019 s.5 bars direct and indirect discrimination against prospective employees and forbids pregnancy questions at recruitment ([WageIndicator Zambia](https://wageindicator.org/en-zm/work-in-zambia/labour-law/fair-treatment)). The Health Professions Act 2024 replaced the 2009 Act, and its reach over industrial psychologists is unclear ([Parliament of Zambia bill](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/bills/N.A.B%2011%20of%202024%2C%20The%20Health%20Professions%20Bill.pdf)).

Botswana's Data Protection Act 2024 replaced the 2018 Act, which never effectively commenced, and keeps the IDPC as regulator ([CIPIT](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/understanding-botswanas-2018-and-2024-data-protection-acts/)). Its commencement date is reported as either 29 October 2024 or 14 January 2025 ([Techhive](https://www.techhiveadvisory.africa/insights/understanding-botswanas-new-data-protection-act-key-updates-and-changes)). The Employment and Labour Relations Act 2025 is reported in force from 1 September 2026 ([ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/botswana-advances-decent-work-landmark-labour-reform)). The Persons with Disability Act 2024 bars disability discrimination in employment ([Act 6 of 2024](https://covid19.gov.bw/sites/default/files/2025-03/Act%20No%206%20of%202024%20PERSONS%20WITH%20DISABILITY%20%281%29.pdf)).

The delivery rules follow from s.8(b) fairness and the data-protection rules. Source evidence on delivery is thin, so all of the following are Basis A. Offer only language versions that the publisher has validated or normed. Never machine-translate items. Log both the test language and the candidate's preferred language. South Africa's reported 12 official languages should be verified.

For low bandwidth, preload items, autosave after each answer, and avoid video by default. Use server-authoritative timers that pause on disconnect, with a grace period and an audit log, so connectivity drops are not penalised. Record device type so that device-related adverse impact can be monitored. Default proctoring should be non-biometric: randomised forms, timestamps and browser focus events. Webcam proctoring counts as special personal data in South Africa and as licensed biometric processing in Zimbabwe, and any flag it raises should go to human review rather than trigger an automatic fail.

## Most candidates are lost before they ever click start

Across more than 200,000 assessment journeys, **start rates ranged from 50.6% (customer service) to 97.4% (utilities)**. Completion among those who started was usually 84–99% ([ThriveMap](https://thrivemap.com/pre-hire-assessment-completion-rates-candidate-drop-off-by-industry/)). Graduate roles started at 78.7% and completed at 90.9%. Customer service roles completed at only 62.4%, and administrative and clerical roles at 72.8%.

HireVue found that most quitters leave in the first few minutes. Cutting a 51-minute assessment to 39 minutes would raise completion by **less than 1%**. About two-thirds of applicants who drop out after applying do so because they **accepted another offer**. About a third of mobile applicants dropped out when the assessment did not work on their device ([HireVue 2025 whitepaper](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025_10_Applicant-Dropout-Completion-Rates_Whitepaper.pdf)). The same paper shows that overstating duration drives dropout, so invitations should quote the median completion time rather than the time limit. For Vettly's high-volume, mobile-heavy markets, the lesson is that mobile compatibility and a fast invitation-to-start step matter more than shaving minutes off the test.

Fairness perceptions also matter for the employer brand. Face validity correlates about .50 with applicant reactions, and perceived fairness correlates **.44 with organisational attractiveness** and .46 with recommendation intentions ([Hausknecht et al. 2004 outline](https://web.pdx.edu/~mccunee/quant_621/Outlines/Hausknecht%202004%20Applicant%20Reactions.doc)). This supports practice items, plain-language instructions and a feedback report for every candidate who completes.

For accommodations, **+25% extra time** is the common default. Guidance warns that it may be insufficient, and lists screen readers, readers or scribes, sign-language interpreters and deadline extensions as further options ([Imperial College guide](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/careers-service/public/about/Psychometric-tests---a-guide-for-disabled-and-neurodivergent-applicants.pdf)).

For security, ITC guidance recommends supervised verification after unproctored testing in moderate- and high-stakes use ([UTC citing ITC](https://utc.edu/document/37431)). Retest gains are real but stop after the third administration ([Scharfen et al. 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intell:v:67:y:2018:i:c:p:44-66)). No source fixes a retest interval, so the 6-month default below is an assumption.

## IPIP is free, but ICAR and HEXACO-PI-R are not

Only one major item pool can safely go into a commercial product. IPIP states that "permission has already been automatically granted for any person to use IPIP items, scales, and inventories for any purpose, commercial or non-commercial" ([IPIP permission page](https://ipip.ori.org/newPermission.htm)). That gives Vettly a lawful source of Big Five and six-factor personality content, provided the scales carry original names and not the proprietary inventory names they proxy.

ICAR, although its 2014 paper described it as public domain, is now distributed under a **Scientific Use License for academic use only**. It may not be used for commercial websites or routine commercial testing, and no commercial licence can be bought ([TU Dortmund ICAR page](https://sms.statistik.tu-dortmund.de/en/research/projects/completed-research-projects/international-cognitive-ability-resource-icar/)). HEXACO-PI-R forms are free only for non-profit academic research, and commercial use requires contacting the authors ([hexaco.org](https://hexaco.org/hexaco-inventory)). Open Psychometrics licenses each measure separately and limits its interactive tests to personal use ([Open Psychometrics](https://openpsychometrics.org/about/)). No open SJT bank was found.

Generic formats such as matrices, number series, analogies, mental rotation and True / False / Cannot Say are not ownable. Specific items, images and item generators are. The cognitive, SJT, integrity and motivation content in the mock data must therefore be newly authored. For the seed database itself, the safest option is to store item *metadata* and a handful of original sample items, not full banks.

On names, proprietary vendors' product and scale names are trademarks and should not appear as Vettly product names. JvR's Southern African catalogue (Talent Basics, Prospect Screener, IWPR, Safety Profile) was confirmed by search ([JvR catalogue](https://jvrpsychometrics.co.za/catalogue/talent-basics-report)). The wider blocklist comes from the researchers' background knowledge and needs a trademark search (CIPC, USPTO, EUIPO). It covers SHL (OPQ32, Verify, MQ, UCF, Great Eight), Saville (Wave, Swift), Hogan (HPI, HDS, MVPI), Korn Ferry / Talent Q (Elements, Dimensions, Aspects, KF4D), Aon / cut-e (ADEPT-15, scales), Criteria (CCAT, UCAT, EPP), Talogy (PAPI, Logiks), Wonderlic, and the trademarks 16PF, MBTI, NEO-PI-R, Everything DiSC, Raven's, Watson-Glaser, Caliper and Predictive Index. In practice, Vettly should avoid the words Verify, Swift, Elements, Dimensions, Wave and scales as product names. It should also avoid alphanumeric patterns like "OPQ32", and keep to descriptive construct labels such as "Numerical reasoning" or "Conscientiousness", which are generic.

## Recommended mock data model

The model below turns the evidence into entities and defaults. Each field's default is either cited (C) or a design assumption (A). Every seeded publisher, product, norm and validity study should be fictional, carry a `is_mock: true` flag, and use Vettly-original names such as "Vettly Data Reasoning", "Vettly Work Styles" or "Vettly Service Judgement".

Seeding volumes should mirror the evidence. A useful starting set is 18–22 tests across the families in the first table, nine norm groups, ten battery templates and four country profiles. Each employer should receive 100–500 invitations, with funnel proportions drawn from the candidate-flow table. Score distributions should reproduce the sten band percentages (2.3 / 4.4 / 9.2 / 15.0 / 19.1% from each tail inward). Group-level ability score gaps in mock adverse-impact dashboards should sit near d ≈ .8 for cognitive tests and near zero for integrity and conscientiousness. That pattern is realistic, and it lets the dashboard show the value of composite batteries.

## Conclusion

Two corrections reshape what a credible Southern African assessment product looks like. The first is psychometric. Since 2022, cognitive ability has been one strong predictor among several rather than the master predictor. A mock catalogue that leads with interviews, work samples and job knowledge, and treats ability as a moderate sift, is both more accurate and less exposed under EEA s.8 fairness tests. The second is legal. Removing HPCSA certification did not lighten the compliance burden; it moved the burden onto evidence, so the validity, norm and bias records attached to each test version are now the compliance artefact. The data model should therefore treat evidence, practitioner, consent and human-review records as entities in their own right, not as metadata.

The unresolved gaps are specific and should be closed before any mock figure becomes product copy. They are: the current HPCSA Form 207 list; the Botswana DPA 2024 provisions and commencement date; the Zimbabwe SI 155 licence tiers and deadline; the Zambian section numbers; the POPIA mappings marked unverified; and any regional norm or subgroup data outside South Africa, of which none was found. Until then, every non-South African norm and fairness figure in the database is a labelled fiction. That labelling is what keeps a realistic mock database honest.
