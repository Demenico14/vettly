## 1. Typical reliability ranges (internal consistency and test-retest) by test type

### Takeaway
Well-built ability tests and Big Five scales typically report alpha / marginal reliability in the .75–.90 range and test-retest around .70–.85; SJTs are the clear outlier, with meta-analytic alphas of roughly .46–.68 because they are multidimensional. Integrity tests sit around alpha .81 / retest .68 (OLDER, 1993).

### Cited Findings
- Big Five meta-analytic internal consistency (Viswesvaran & Ones, 2000; 1,359 alphas): .78 (SD .11, K=370), .78 (SD .09, K=307), .73 (SD .12, K=251), .75 (SD .11, K=123), .78 (SD .10, K=307); mean stability (test-retest) coefficients .75, .76, .71, .69, .72. Trait dimension does not strongly moderate reliability. (OLDER: 2000) — [UMN Experts record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/measurement-error-in-big-five-factors-personality-assessment-reli/)
- BFI / BFI-2 reliability generalization (Husain et al., 2025, BMC Psychology; 34 articles, ~43,715 participants): BFI pooled alpha openness .77, conscientiousness .80, extraversion .80; BFI-2 higher on most factors. — [DOAJ / BMC Psychology 2025](https://doaj.org/article/b9a8ff5ddbaa489699a5f059777e4c93)
- SJT internal consistency: Catano, Brochu & Lamerson (2012) meta-analysis of 56 alphas from 39 studies (N=45,062): meta-analytic alpha = .46 ("on the whole quite poor"). — [Goldsmiths eprint citing Catano et al.](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/20393/1/IJT_Jan_17.docx)
- SJT reliability generalization (Kasten & Freund, 2016, European Journal of Psychological Assessment 32(3), 230–240): concluded SJT score reliability "typically rather low" and below recommended levels for high-stakes use; a secondary source cites their average alpha as .68 across 271 SJTs (could not verify in full text). — [Leuphana record](https://fis.leuphana.de/en/publications/a-meta-analytical-multilevel-reliability-generalization-of-situat/); [arXiv 2412.12144 citing .68](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.12144). Conflict: .46 (Catano) vs .68 (Kasten & Freund) — difference likely reflects sample of SJTs and multilevel method.
- Integrity tests (Ones, 1993 report associated with Ones, Viswesvaran & Schmidt, 1993): mean alpha .81 (SD .10); overt tests .82 (SD .09); personality-based tests .77; mean test-retest .68 (SD .12). (OLDER: 1993; pre-publication report) — [DTIC ADA254681](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA254681.pdf)
- Example contemporary online reasoning battery (verbal/numerical/inductive, 32 items, 372 sittings): total alpha .877 (95% CI .858–.894); test-retest not yet reported; norms tables only where n ≥ 300. Single verbal test: alpha .800 (.781–.817), n=1,468, 25 items. (Small vendor; illustrative of short-form magnitudes.) — [JobCannon timed reasoning manual](https://jobcannon.io/research/technical-manuals/shl-practice-test); [JobCannon verbal manual](https://jobcannon.io/research/technical-manuals/verbal-reasoning)
- Saville Swift Analysis (verbal & numerical combined) technical summary reports N=28,647 with SEm 3.61 (2022). — [Saville technical summary (search snippet; PDF since 404)](https://www.savilleassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Analysis-Aptitude-Range-Technical-Summary.pdf)
- Retest effects (relevant to retest policy, not reliability): Scharfen, Peters & Holling (2018, Intelligence) — 174 samples, 786 outcomes, N=153,185; significant score gains on retest, no further gains after the third administration; moderated by content, form equivalence, interval and age. — [RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intell:v:67:y:2018:i:c:p:44-66)
- Sackett et al. (2022) Table 5 assumes predictor reliability .80 for cognitive ability tests as a planning value. — [Sackett et al. 2022 accepted manuscript](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)

### Inferences
- Short adaptive/IRT forms (10–20 items) typically trade a few points of reliability for time; marginal reliability around .80 is the realistic floor vendors advertise.
- For speeded clerical tests (error checking), alpha overstates reliability; a credible mock manual would report split-half with Spearman–Brown or retest.
- For SJTs a credible mock manual should report alpha ~.50–.70 with an explicit note that test-retest or parallel-form reliability is the more appropriate index.

### Gaps
- No primary meta-analytic reliability figures retrieved for mechanical, spatial, error-checking, EI (trait vs ability) or motivation questionnaires; ranges in table for those rows are inferred from general practice, not sourced.
- Full Kasten & Freund (2016) average alpha not verified in primary text.
- SHL/Saville/Clevry South African technical manuals (e.g. Clevry "South Africa Technical Information 2021-23") could not be opened.

## 2. Meta-analytic predictive validity: Schmidt & Hunter (1998) vs Sackett et al. (2022); incremental validity; subgroup differences

### Takeaway
Sackett et al. (2022) argue that range-restriction overcorrection inflated earlier estimates; validities fall by roughly .10–.20 for most predictors and structured interviews (.42) become the top predictor, with cognitive ability dropping from .51 to .31. Black–White d for cognitive ability is .79 vs .10 for integrity, −.07 for conscientiousness and .23 for structured interviews, so composites reduce adverse impact.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Sackett, P. R., Zhang, C., Berry, C. M., & Lievens, F. (2022). Revisiting meta-analytic estimates of validity in personnel selection: Addressing systematic overcorrection for restriction of range. Journal of Applied Psychology, 107(11), 2040–2068. doi:10.1037/apl0000994. Criterion reliability assumed .60 (supervisor ratings). — [SMU repository](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894); [accepted manuscript PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)
- Full Table 3 of Sackett et al. (2022), transcribed from the accepted manuscript (S&H = Schmidt & Hunter 1998; blank = not in S&H): — [Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)

- Interests rose from .10 to .24 when defined as person–job fit; contextualized personality markedly stronger than general personality. — [SIOP TIP article](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)
- Integrity original estimate: Ones, Viswesvaran & Schmidt (1993) JAP 78(4), 679–703; 665 validity coefficients, N=576,460; operational validity .41 for supervisory ratings (OLDER). — [UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/comprehensive-meta-analysis-of-integrity-test-validities-findings/); [Schmidt NIDA monograph](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090813170431/http:/drugabuse.gov/pdf/monographs/monograph170/069-095_Schmidt.pdf)
- Incremental validity (pre-Sackett framework): Schmidt, Oh & Shaffer (2016 working paper) report highest composite validity for GMA + integrity (.78) and GMA + structured interview (.76), with GMA alone revised upward to .65 (vs .51 in 1998); S&H 1998 gave GMA + integrity as .65. These composite figures derive from the corrections Sackett et al. criticise and come from secondary summaries — treat as upper bounds. — [Stafforini summary](https://stafforini.com/works/schmidt-2016-validity-and-utility/); [Master-HR comparison](https://master-hr.com/insights/new-study-providing-updated-validity-estimates/)

### South African evidence on group differences
- Odendaal (2015, SAJIP; N=1,640 SA job applicants): race and language differences in social desirability not fully explained by cognitive ability; cultural factors play a role; notes well-constructed personality instruments likely cause less adverse impact than cognitive tests. — [DOAJ / SAJIP 2015](https://doaj.org/article/6f424450ed2f4f0f884d027ccea27751)
- Stellenbosch dissertation (2011) on cognitive norms: on language/education-sensitive measures, performance ordered consistently with English-speaking, white, advantaged groups highest; education quality a strong driver; local norms often fail to reflect SA linguistic diversity. — [Stellenbosch thesis](https://scholar.sun.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/ed98c8d4-48f7-4b79-801b-e3e232c6a252/content)
- Cockcroft et al. (2015): WAIS-III factor structures differ between SA and UK samples; UK outperformed on knowledge-based verbal subtests, SA better on processing speed; norms predominantly from monolingual, affluent, English-first-language individuals. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00297/full)
- OLDER (1994) secondary-school study reported Black–White mean differences of ~2 SD in SA (student, not applicant, sample; not comparable to selection data). — [Journal of General Psychology 1994 abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00221309.1994.9711170)
- Nonverbal tests are not automatically bias-free: SAJIP DIF study across five SA language groups. — [SAJIP article 881](https://sajip.co.za/index.php/sajip/article/view/881/1101)
- Legal context: Employment Equity Act s.8 requires psychological tests to be scientifically shown valid and reliable, applied fairly to all employees, and not biased against any employee or group. The 2014 s.8(d) HPCSA-certification requirement was struck down by the High Court (ATPSA challenge) and recorded as deleted by Employment Equity Amendment Act 4 of 2022, effective 1 January 2025 (unofficial consolidation; verify in Gazette). — [Acts Online s.8](https://www.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php); [Webber Wentzel](https://www.webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/psychological-testing-and-other-similar-assessments-of-employees-an-update.aspx); [SA Labour News](https://salabournews.co.za/37376-court-cancels-proviso-on-hpcsa-certification-of-psychological-tests-for-jobs)

### Inferences
- For a mock catalogue, quote Sackett 2022 values as the headline "operational validity" with S&H 1998 as "historical"; GMA ~.31, SJT ~.26, conscientiousness ~.19 are defensible 2025–2026 figures.
- Plausible mock "battery composite" validities under the Sackett framework would be ~.40–.50 (e.g. structured interview + cognitive + integrity), not the .76–.78 of Schmidt et al. 2016.
- In SA, cognitive-test group differences are likely at least as large as US d≈.79 and strongly confounded with language of testing and schooling quality, which is why SA practitioners emphasise local norms, DIF analysis and multi-method batteries.

### Gaps
- No SA applicant-sample meta-analysis of Black–White (or African–White) d for cognitive, personality or SJTs was found; no Zimbabwe, Zambia or Botswana group-difference evidence found.
- Joseph & Newman (2010) EI validity values (ability vs mixed) were not retrieved from primary source; Sackett 2022 values (.22 ability; .30 personality-based) used instead.
- McDaniel et al. (2007) SJT values (S&H-era ~.26 knowledge vs behavioural tendency) not separately verified; Sackett 2022 reports .26 for both.
- Schmidt, Oh & Shaffer (2016) full Table 1 (incremental R per predictor) not accessible (PDF 404).

## 3. Norm groups: definitions, sample sizes, refresh cycles, local vs international

### Takeaway
Vendors segment norms by job level (general/graduate/professional/manager-executive/operational), sometimes by industry, and by country/region; SA-specific norms exist for major vendors (e.g. a South African manager composite for numerical reasoning; Clevry SA technical data 2021–23). Public data on sample sizes and refresh cycles is thin.

### Cited Findings
- SHL Verify Interactive norm list (updated January 2024) includes "Numerical Reasoning Manager Composite (South Africa)" alongside Middle East and China norms — i.e. SA norms are offered as level-specific composites. — [SHL support document](https://support.shl.com/documents/987/attachments/5516)
- Clevry publishes a "South Africa Technical Information 2021-23" document covering SA norm group data for all ability tests and personality scale technical information (page last updated June 2024) — evidence of ~2–3-year norm data windows. — [Clevry SA technical data](https://help.clevry.com/south-african-technical-data)
- Eras aptitude tests are levelled into Managerial & Professional and Supervisory & Team Leader versions, plus specialist inductive reasoning. (2016) — [Eras aptitude tests PDF](https://feeds.jgp.co.uk/public_data/VacancyDocuments/0074/4151/Eras_aptitude_tests.pdf)
- Example minimum norm size policy: a separate language-specific norm table only where n ≥ 300; otherwise a pooled all-language norm. — [JobCannon aptitude manual](https://jobcannon.io/research/technical-manuals/aptitude-test)
- Saville Swift Analysis norm-based reliability sample N=28,647 (illustrates large international pooled samples). — [Saville technical summary](https://www.savilleassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Analysis-Aptitude-Range-Technical-Summary.pdf)
- JvR Psychometrics publishes South African supplements to international manuals (e.g. MAS SA supplement). — [JvR MAS SA supplement](https://manuals.jvrpsychometrics.co.za/assets/MAS-SA-supplement.pdf)
- SA norms for many tests "often don't reflect the ethnic and linguistic diversity" of the country; for some tests other-population norms were suitable. — [Stellenbosch thesis](https://scholar.sun.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/ed98c8d4-48f7-4b79-801b-e3e232c6a252/content)

### Inferences
- Norm sizes of 300 (minimum) to 1,000+ per group are realistic; international pooled norms reach 10,000–30,000.

- Use local (SA/regional) norms when candidate pool is local and test is language-sensitive (verbal, numerical word problems); international norms are defensible for multinational/graduate pools or where measurement invariance has been shown. Report demographic composition (gender, race, home language, education) for every SA norm, given EEA s.8 fairness obligations.

## 4. Role-to-battery mappings and typical durations

### Takeaway
Public sources only confirm level-based packaging (managerial/professional vs supervisory/operational); detailed role mappings below are inferred from the validity evidence and common vendor packaging and should be presented as illustrative.

### Cited Findings
- Level-based test versions (Managerial & Professional; Supervisory & Team Leader) with specialist inductive reasoning. — [Eras aptitude tests PDF](https://feeds.jgp.co.uk/public_data/VacancyDocuments/0074/4151/Eras_aptitude_tests.pdf)
- Work samples and job knowledge tests suit roles where applicants must already be trained/experienced (e.g. electrician, mechanic); cognitive tests suit untrained applicants who will learn on the job. — [Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)
- SA numerical norm offered as manager composite. — [SHL support document](https://support.shl.com/documents/987/attachments/5516)

### Illustrative role-to-battery table (inferred)

| Role family | Typical battery | Approx. total time |
|---|---|---|
| Graduate programme | Numerical + verbal + inductive (adaptive, ~15–20 min each) + work-styles personality + SJT | 60–90 min |
| Professional / specialist | Verbal + numerical (prof. level) + personality; job knowledge test where applicable | 50–75 min |
| Managers / leaders | Verbal + numerical (manager norm) + personality + motivation; SJT or assessment centre exercises for senior | 60–90 min (AC: half to full day) |
| Technical / engineering | Mechanical + spatial + numerical (+ abstract); job knowledge / work sample | 45–75 min |
| Customer service / contact centre | Customer-service SJT + verbal comprehension + error checking + work-styles | 35–50 min |
| Finance / accounting | Numerical (high difficulty) + error checking/data checking + verbal + integrity/conscientiousness | 45–65 min |
| Sales | Sales SJT + personality (extraversion, achievement) + motivation + verbal | 40–60 min |
| Operations / supervisory | Numerical + verbal (supervisory level) + SJT + personality | 45–60 min |
| Administrative / clerical | Error checking + verbal + numerical (basic) + integrity | 30–45 min |
| IT / software | Abstract/inductive + numerical + coding work sample / knowledge test | 60–120 min |

### Inferences
- Graduate 60–90 min and admin 30–45 min reflect common candidate-experience limits; adaptive tests let vendors keep each reasoning test to 12–20 min.
- Adding integrity and personality to ability helps both validity and adverse impact (low d values in Table 3).

### Gaps
- No public vendor documentation found specifying role-family battery templates and durations; table is inference.

## 5. Recommended use: screen-out vs inform-interview, cut-scores vs top-down, combining with interviews, weighting vs CV evidence

### Takeaway
The 2022 evidence repositions the structured interview as the focal predictor and recommends multi-component systems; tests with large group differences are best used with moderate cut-scores or as one weighted input rather than strict top-down ranking, especially under SA EEA s.8.

### Cited Findings
- Structured interviews proposed as focal predictor; job-specific measures strongest; use combinations of predictors to reduce group differences. — [Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)
- Unstructured interviews .19 vs structured .42; years of experience .07; education excluded (.10 S&H). Implies CV-derived experience/education signals should carry low weight relative to tests and structured interviews. — [Sackett et al. 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2021-sackett.pdf)
- EEA s.8: tests must be valid, reliable, fairly applicable and unbiased. — [Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php)
- Retest gains plateau after the third administration; equivalent forms and intervals moderate gains (relevant to retest policies for screen-out tests). — [RePEc / Scharfen et al. 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intell:v:67:y:2018:i:c:p:44-66)

### Inferences
- Typical practice for a mock catalogue: unsupervised online ability tests used as a sift at a low-to-moderate cut (e.g. 30th–40th percentile on the relevant norm), followed by supervised verification or structured interview; personality/motivation used to inform interview probes rather than as screen-out.
- Suggested weighting example: structured interview 35–40%, ability 25–30%, work sample/job knowledge 15–20%, personality/SJT 10–15%, CV screen (experience/qualifications) as minimum-requirement gate only.
- Top-down ranking on cognitive scores maximises utility but increases adverse impact (d≈.79); banding or composite scoring mitigates this.
- Retest policy: 6–12 month wait or alternate form, given documented retest effects.

### Gaps
- SIOP Principles (2018) and ITC Guidelines on Test Use text not retrieved; recommendations above are not directly quoted from them.
- No SA-specific empirical studies on cut-score practice found.
