# Workplace Psychometric Assessment Types, Formats and Scoring Outputs

Purpose: factual reference for designing an ORIGINAL mock catalogue of workplace tests. Vendor names appear only for factual context (counts and timings). No proprietary items are reproduced. Trademarked product or scale names are not to be reused in the mock catalogue. Use generic labels instead.

Source quality note: official publisher pages and fact sheets, academic papers and open-source projects (ICAR, HEXACO, IPIP) are treated as primary. Test-prep sites (assessmentday, jobtestprep, practiceaptitudetests, prepterminal, careertestprep, jobcannon) are secondary. They are used only to corroborate typical timings and are flagged where used. Several official SHL PDFs could not be machine-read (compressed PDF streams), so some SHL figures come via secondary reporting.

## 1. Constructs and sub-scales measured by each assessment type

### Takeaway
Workplace batteries fall into three groups. Maximum-performance tests are cognitive or aptitude tests with right and wrong answers. Typical-performance self-report covers personality, values, motivation and trait EI. Hybrid or job-specific measures include SJTs, integrity tests, work samples, job knowledge, typing and language. Cognitive tests are usually built from a few item families (series, matrices, verbal, spatial) that combine into a general ability ("g") composite. Personality tools are built on the Five-Factor or HEXACO models, or on broader occupational "work styles" models with about 30 scales in 3–4 domains.

### Cited Findings

**General cognitive ability (GMA / "g")**
- The open-source ICAR-60 has 4 item types: 9 Letter and Number Series, 11 Matrix Reasoning, 16 Verbal Reasoning and 24 Three-dimensional Rotation items. It was validated on about 97,000 participants. — [Condon & Revelle 2014, ICAR paper](https://personality-project.org/revelle/publications/condon.icar.14.pdf); [ICAR project overview (TU Dortmund)](https://sms.statistik.tu-dortmund.de/en/research/projects/completed-research-projects/international-cognitive-ability-resource-icar/)
- Additional public-domain ICAR item types include figural analogies, number series, perceptual maze and propositional reasoning. A 12-item ICAR "sample test" exists as a brief measure. ICAR is restricted to academic use. — [ICAR project (TU Dortmund)](https://sms.statistik.tu-dortmund.de/en/research/projects/completed-research-projects/international-cognitive-ability-resource-icar/); [personalityresearch.org](https://personalityresearch.org/)
- In occupational settings, a vendor states that "2–4 aptitude tests" are enough for an adequately g-loaded aggregate score. — [Test Partnership, assessment theory](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/test-partnership-assessments-theory.html)

**Numerical reasoning** (sub-types: data/table/chart interpretation, number series, calculation/estimation)
- One SHL interactive numerical fact sheet gives 18 minutes and a maximum of 10 questions. SHL's adaptive numerical ability fact sheet gives a maximum of 16 questions, multiple-choice and adaptive. The interactive "calculation" variant gives 10 minutes and a maximum of 12 questions. These figures were reported via search summaries of the SHL fact sheets, because the PDFs could not be parsed directly. — [SHL Verify Interactive Numerical factsheet](https://www.shl.com/assets/documents/rebranded-assets/product-factsheet-verify-interactive-numerical-reasoning.pdf); [SHL Verify Numerical Ability factsheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/Verify_Numerical_Ability_Fact_Sheet.pdf)
- Prep-site ranges for graduate numerical tests: 18–25 multiple-choice questions in 17–35 minutes. Another source gives "12–30 minutes". — [AssessmentDay SHL numerical](https://assessmentday.co.uk/shl-numerical-reasoning-test.htm); [AssessmentDay SHL](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/shl.htm)
- A Talent Q (now Korn Ferry) numerical test is reported at 12 questions in about 15 minutes, with per-item time limits of about 75 seconds across 4 sections. This is a secondary source. — [Assessment-Training blog](https://blog.assessment-training.com/aptitude-tests/numerical-reasoning-shl-tests)

**Verbal reasoning** (sub-types: True/False/Cannot Say comprehension, verbal analogies, vocabulary, grammar/spelling)
- The dominant format is a short passage followed by statements, each judged as True, False or Cannot Say on the passage alone. "Cannot Say" is the answer when the passage neither confirms nor refutes the statement. — [practiceaptitudetests TFCS guide](https://practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/verbal-reasoning-test-practice-true-false-cannot-say)
- Typical published lengths: 20 questions in 20 minutes on one vendor's page, and 9 questions in 10 minutes on another. — [practiceaptitudetests verbal](https://practiceaptitudetests.com/verbal-reasoning-tests); [AssessmentDay free verbal test](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/verbal/Free/Verbal-Reasoning-Test1-Questions.pdf)
- Critical-thinking variant: one publisher's critical-thinking appraisal is 40 items, timed at 30–40 minutes, with 5 subtests: drawing inferences, recognising assumptions, deduction, interpretation and evaluating arguments. — [TalentLens (Pearson)](https://www.talentlens.com/recruitment/assessments/watson-glaser-critical-thinking-appraisal.html)

**Abstract / inductive / logical / deductive / diagrammatic reasoning**
- ICAR matrix reasoning and figural analogies are public-domain models of the inductive or abstract item family. — [ICAR paper](https://personality-project.org/revelle/publications/condon.icar.14.pdf)
- Deductive reasoning in critical-thinking tests uses premises followed by a "conclusion follows / does not follow" judgement. — [TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/recruitment/assessments/watson-glaser-critical-thinking-appraisal.html)

**Mechanical reasoning**
- The classic mechanical comprehension test has 68 items in 30 minutes. Its second edition has 55 items in 25 minutes. Content covers levers, pulleys, gears, forces, fluids and electricity in illustrated multiple-choice items. — [Wikipedia: Mechanical aptitude](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_aptitude); [practiceaptitudetests Bennett](https://practiceaptitudetests.com/bennett-mechanical-comprehension-tests)

**Spatial reasoning**
- ICAR's three-dimensional rotation (24 items in the ICAR-60) is the public-domain model: mental rotation of a cube with marked faces. — [ICAR paper](https://personality-project.org/revelle/publications/condon.icar.14.pdf)

**Error checking / attention to detail / checking**
- Checking tests compare short strings (codes, names, numbers, addresses) against a source to spot discrepancies. Time limits are "very demanding", with a few seconds per item. One sample paper has 40 items in 15 minutes, about 22 seconds per item. — [AssessmentDay attention to detail](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/resources/attention-to-detail-test.html); [practiceaptitudetests attention to detail](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/attention-to-detail-test/)

**Personality – Five-Factor Model and HEXACO**
- The HEXACO-PI-R has 6 factors with 4 facets each (24 facets), plus an interstitial Altruism scale (25 scales in total). It exists as a 200-item full form, a 100-item form and a 60-item short form. Altruism is excluded when scoring the 6 factors. — [hexaco.org history](https://hexaco.org/history); [CASRAI HEXACO guide](https://casrai.org/guides/hexaco)
- The Honesty-Humility facets are Sincerity, Fairness, Greed Avoidance and Modesty. — [hexaco.org](https://hexaco.org/history); [replicationindex HEXACO-100](https://replicationindex.com/2020/10/11/a-big-5-model-of-the-hexaco-100-items/)
- HEXACO-100: 4 items per facet × 24 facets = 96 items, plus 4 Altruism items. — [replicationindex](https://replicationindex.com/2020/10/11/a-big-5-model-of-the-hexaco-100-items/)

**Personality – occupational "work styles" questionnaires (~25–32 scales)**
- The best-known occupational questionnaire has 32 scales grouped into 3 domains: relationships with people, thinking style, and feelings and emotions. Some descriptions add a fourth "dynamism/energy" grouping. Its current version has 104 forced-choice blocks scored by IRT. Its normative version has about 230 items on a 5-point rating scale. — [SHL OPQ product fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf); [Brown & Bartram, Univ. of Kent](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/id/document/3179616); [careertestprep OPQ guide (secondary)](https://www.careertestprep.com/blog/opq32-personality-test-guide)
- Sources conflict on block size. Most describe triads (3 statements, pick "most" and "least" like me, 312 statements in all). Other accounts of earlier ipsative versions describe quads. — [prepterminal (secondary)](https://www.prepterminal.com/shl-personality-test); [careertestprep (secondary)](https://www.careertestprep.com/opq32)

**Situational judgement tests (SJTs)**
- Meta-analytic corrected validities against job performance are .26–.34. SJTs add 1–2% variance over cognitive ability and the Big Five. Response instruction type matters: "would do" (behavioural tendency) vs "should do" (knowledge). — [McDaniel et al. 2007, Personnel Psychology, via review](https://typeset.io/pdf/situational-judgment-tests-a-review-of-practice-and-28k2pes4mu.pdf); [McDaniel, Psotka & Legree 2009](https://mcdaniel97.github.io/Publications/McDaniel,%20Psotka%20&%20Legree%20(2009,%20SIOP).pdf)
- A large-sample study (N = 31,194 applicants) compared rate, rank and most/least formats on construct validity, subgroup differences and reliability. Rank and most/least formats behaved similarly. — [core.ac.uk SJT response-format study](https://core.ac.uk/download/55855017.pdf)
- Common formats: pick most and least likely or effective; rate each option's effectiveness (e.g. 1–4 or 1–5); rank all options; or select a single best response. — [practiceaptitudetests SJT formats](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/situational-judgement-test-response-formats/)

**Integrity / counterproductive work behaviour (CWB)**
- Overt tests ask directly about attitudes to theft and dishonesty and admissions of past behaviour, and make no attempt to hide their purpose. Personality-based tests infer integrity from conscientiousness, agreeableness and emotional stability-type items. — [US OPM integrity/honesty tests](https://opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/integrityhonesty-tests); [QIC-WD Integrity Tests summary](https://www.qic-wd.org/sites/default/files/Integrity%20Tests%20Umbrella%20Summary%20040120.pdf)
- Ones, Viswesvaran & Schmidt (1993) meta-analysed 665 validity studies. For CWB other than theft, validity was .39 for overt tests and .29 for personality-based tests. For theft, it was .33 for both. — [DTIC report](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA254681.pdf); [QIC-WD summary](https://qic-wd.org/node/457)
- Faking is a particular concern with overt tests. — [OPM](https://opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/integrityhonesty-tests)

**Emotional intelligence: ability-based vs trait**
- The ability model (MSCEIT) has 141 items across 8 tasks in 4 branches: perceiving, using, understanding and managing emotions. It takes 30–45 minutes and yields 15 scores (total, 2 areas, 4 branches, 8 tasks). It is scored against consensus or expert keys, not self-rating. — [EI Consortium: MSCEIT](https://eiconsortium.org/measures/msceit.html)
- The trait model (TEIQue) is self-report: 153 items, 15 facets, 4 factors (wellbeing, self-control, emotionality, sociability) plus global trait EI, in about 25 minutes. — [EI Consortium: TEIQue](https://eiconsortium.org/measures/teique.html); [Psychometric Lab](https://old.psychometriclab.com/obtaining-the-teique/)

**Typing speed and accuracy**
- Output is gross WPM (all words) and net WPM (errors deducted). The standard word is 5 characters. Net WPM = gross WPM − (errors ÷ minutes). Employment tests run 3–5 minutes, and some employers accept only tests of 5 minutes or longer. Accuracy is typically expected at 95% or higher. Benchmarks vary: 35–55 WPM in one source, 50–60 WPM in another. — [EmployTest](https://employtest.com/typing-test-for-employment/); [Canditech](https://www.canditech.io/blog/what-is-a-good-typing-speed/); [typingtest.now (secondary)](https://typingtest.now/blog/ultimate-guide-passing-typing-test-2026)

**Comparative validity (context for catalogue "predictive power" claims)**
- Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022, JAP 107(11), 2040–2068) found mean validities .10–.20 lower than earlier estimates, because earlier range-restriction corrections were often inappropriate. Structured interviews now rank first, followed by job knowledge and empirically keyed biodata. Cognitive ability plays a "more modest role". Combined batteries reach about .47. — [SMU repository](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894); [Sackett et al. 2023 I-O Perspectives](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/S175494262300024Xa.pdf/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors.pdf); [TestGorilla summary (vendor)](https://www.testgorilla.com/blog/hiring-tools-validity-revisited)

## 2. Item counts and time limits across vendors

### Takeaway
Aptitude tests are short "power-with-time-pressure" tests: roughly 10–25 items in 10–25 minutes, about 45–90 seconds per item. Adaptive versions use fewer items. Checking tests are explicitly speeded at a few seconds per item. Self-report questionnaires are untimed, at about 100–230 items and 25–45 minutes. SJTs typically have 15–30 scenarios.

### Cited Findings
- Numerical: about 10–16 items (adaptive) in about 18 minutes per SHL fact sheets, and 18–25 items in 17–35 minutes per prep-site ranges. — [SHL factsheet](https://www.shl.com/assets/documents/rebranded-assets/product-factsheet-verify-interactive-numerical-reasoning.pdf); [AssessmentDay](https://assessmentday.co.uk/shl-numerical-reasoning-test.htm)
- Combined SHL "general ability" test: about 8–10 numerical items within a 36-minute total (secondary source). — [JobTestPrep SHL general ability](https://jobtestprep.co.uk/shl-general-ability)
- Critical thinking: 40 items in 30–40 minutes, about 45 seconds per item. — [TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/recruitment/assessments/watson-glaser-critical-thinking-appraisal.html)
- Mechanical: 68 items in 30 minutes, or 55 items in 25 minutes. — [Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_aptitude)
- Checking: for example, 40 items in 15 minutes. — [AssessmentDay](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/resources/attention-to-detail-test.html)
- Work-styles personality: 104 triads (about 312 statements) for forced-choice, or about 230 Likert items for normative. — [SHL OPQ fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf); [careertestprep (secondary)](https://www.careertestprep.com/blog/opq32-personality-test-guide)
- HEXACO: 60, 100 or 200 items. — [hexaco.org](https://hexaco.org/history)
- EI: 141 items in 30–45 minutes (ability), 153 items in about 25 minutes (trait). — [EI Consortium MSCEIT](https://eiconsortium.org/measures/msceit.html); [EI Consortium TEIQue](https://eiconsortium.org/measures/teique.html)
- Typing: 3–5 minutes. — [EmployTest](https://employtest.com/typing-test-for-employment/)

### Inferences
- Seconds-per-item heuristics for the mock catalogue: numerical 60–90 s; verbal 45–60 s; abstract/inductive 45–60 s; critical thinking about 45 s; mechanical about 26–30 s; checking 5–25 s; personality about 8–12 s per statement (untimed).
- Some vendors (for example the reported Talent Q design) apply per-item timers instead of a single section timer. The mock catalogue could model both a section timer and a per-item timer.

### Gaps
- SJT scenario counts (15–30) and timings came from the brief and general practice. No primary vendor figure was retrieved this session.

## 3. Fixed vs computer-adaptive delivery; item banks; speeded vs power tests

### Takeaway
Modern cognitive tests are increasingly IRT-calibrated and computer-adaptive (CAT), drawing from large item banks. Each candidate gets a different, shorter test. Personality forced-choice tools now use Thurstonian IRT to recover normative scores.

### Cited Findings
- In CAT, difficulty adjusts to the candidate's answers, which gives shorter tests without loss of accuracy. Each candidate gets a different item set, which improves security. — [Test Partnership adaptive tests](https://testpartnership.com/adaptive-tests.html)
- Item banks require "hundreds of questions". New items are trialled with at least 250–500 participants per item to estimate IRT difficulty before CAT use. — [Test Partnership assessment theory](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/test-partnership-assessments-theory.html)
- IRT scoring accounts for item difficulty instead of just counting correct answers. — [Test Partnership science](https://testpartnership.com/science.html)
- SHL's adaptive numerical test is described as "adaptive" with a maximum item count, not a fixed form. — [SHL Verify Numerical Ability factsheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/Verify_Numerical_Ability_Fact_Sheet.pdf)
- Checking tests are speeded: "a few seconds" per discrepancy. — [AssessmentDay](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/resources/attention-to-detail-test.html)

### Inferences
- Speeded tests have easy items, and the score depends on how many are completed accurately (checking, typing, clerical). Power tests have increasing difficulty and generous time (critical thinking, some untimed ability tests). Most graduate aptitude tests are hybrids.
- For a mock catalogue, each test record could carry these fields: `delivery: fixed | randomised_parallel_form | adaptive`, `timing: section | per_item | untimed`, `test_mode: speed | power | hybrid`, `item_bank_size`, `max_items`.
- Negative marking is rare in these tests. — [practiceaptitudetests verbal tips](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/verbal-reasoning-tips-and-tricks/)

### Gaps
- Specific CAT stopping rules (standard-error thresholds) and actual vendor bank sizes were not found publicly.

## 4. Item formats: normative Likert, ipsative/forced-choice, partially ipsative

### Takeaway
Likert (normative) items are quick and allow between-person comparison but are easy to fake. Forced-choice blocks (pairs, triads, quads; most/least like me) resist faking because the statements within a block are matched on social desirability. Classical scoring of forced-choice blocks produces ipsative data, where scores sum to a constant and only within-person comparison is valid. Thurstonian IRT (or MUPP) models recover approximately normative scores.

### Cited Findings
- Forced-choice formats are argued to resist faking better than rating scales, but traditional scoring yields ipsative data, which has limited their use. — [Brown & Maydeu-Olivares, PMC4923072](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4923072)
- Thurstonian IRT and MUPP are the two leading IRT models for normative scoring of forced-choice responses. They enable the between-person comparison needed in selection. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00806/pdf); [Fisher et al. 2019, core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/234763786.pdf)
- Statements within a block must be matched on desirability. Thurstonian IRT is best suited to tests measuring many traits. — [MDPI Behavioral Sciences 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/14/12/1118)
- IRT scoring reduces ipsativity relative to summative scoring, but does not fully eliminate it unless some blocks are unbalanced. — [Missouri State, "It's All Relative" (2026)](https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/articles00/36)
- The validity advantage of Thurstonian IRT over classical scoring has been inconsistent. — [Fisher et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/234763786.pdf)
- Graded paired comparisons (rating the relative preference within a pair on a multi-point scale) are a less-ipsative alternative. — [Organizational Research Methods 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10944281241279790)
- The best-known work-styles tool moved from an ipsative quad version to an IRT-scored forced-choice triad version, with a separate normative Likert version. — [Brown & Bartram, Univ. of Kent](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/id/document/3179616); [SHL OPQ fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf)

### Inferences

| Format | Example structure | Scoring | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normative Likert | 1–5 "Strongly disagree" to "Strongly agree" (sometimes 1–7) | Sum/mean → norm | Fast, between-person comparison | Faking, acquiescence |
| Fully ipsative forced-choice | Quad: choose most and least like me | Rank points per scale; scale scores sum to a constant | Faking-resistant | Within-person only; distorts correlations |
| Partially ipsative | Rate each statement and also choose most/least, or blocks with unequal scale coverage | Hybrid | Compromise | Complex |
| IRT forced-choice | Triads/pairs, Thurstonian IRT | θ estimates → norm | Faking-resistant, normative | Needs large calibration, complex |

### Gaps
- No quantitative meta-estimate of how much faking is reduced (for example, an effect size under applicant vs honest instructions) was retrieved.

## 5. Scoring outputs: definitions and band cut-offs

### Takeaway
Raw scores are converted against a norm group into percentiles and standard scores. The conventions are: z (mean 0, SD 1); T (mean 50, SD 10); sten (1–10, mean 5.5, SD 2, each band ½ SD wide); stanine (1–9, mean 5, SD about 2). UK occupational reports most often use stens for personality and percentiles or 5-band grades for ability.

### Cited Findings
- T-score = 50 + 10z, typical range 20–80. T = 60 is about the 84th percentile. T = 30 is about the 2nd percentile. — [arXiv psychometrics guidelines](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09959); [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/praxis-plt-7-12/standardized-testing-scores-data/score-types-statistics)
- Sten: 1–10, mean 5.5, SD 2. Each sten is ½ SD wide, and sten 1 is everything below z = −2.0. It was introduced by Canfield (1951) and is standard in UK and Irish occupational testing. — [Wikipedia: Sten scores](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sten_scores); [Test Partnership: sten](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/what-is-a-sten-score.html)
- Expected percentage of the population per sten band (1→10): 2.28, 4.41, 9.18, 14.99, 19.15, 19.15, 14.99, 9.18, 4.41, 2.28. Band mid-point percentiles: 1.14, 4.48, 11.27, 23.36, 40.43, 59.57, 76.64, 88.73, 95.52, 98.86. — [Wikipedia: Sten scores](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sten_scores)
- Stanine: 1–9, mean 5, SD about 2. Cumulative percentage cut-offs are 4, 11, 23, 40, 60, 77, 89, 96 (band sizes 4-7-12-17-20-17-12-7-4%). Bands 1–3 are "low", 4–6 "average", 7–9 "high". — [Test Partnership: stanine](https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/what-is-a-stanine-score.html); [psyctc.org](https://www.psyctc.org/psyctc/?p=4802)
- Conversions assume normality. Skewed distributions require truncation, and table-based conversions only approximate the target percentages. — [Wikipedia: Sten scores](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sten_scores)

### Inferences

Conversion formulas, for implementation:
- z = (raw − norm_mean) / norm_SD
- T = 50 + 10z (clip to 20–80)
- sten = clamp(round(2z + 5.5), 1, 10). Equivalent cut-offs on z: <−2.0 →1; −2.0 to −1.5 →2; −1.5 to −1.0 →3; −1.0 to −0.5 →4; −0.5 to 0 →5; 0 to 0.5 →6; 0.5 to 1.0 →7; 1.0 to 1.5 →8; 1.5 to 2.0 →9; >2.0 →10
- stanine = clamp(round(2z + 5), 1, 9). Cut-offs on z at ±0.25, ±0.75, ±1.25, ±1.75
- percentile = Φ(z) × 100 (normal approximation), or the empirical rank in the norm group

| Sten | z range | Cum. % at upper edge | Typical label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | < −2.0 | 2.3 | Very low |

- Composite or overall scores: average the z (or T) scores of the component tests, optionally weighted by job relevance, then re-standardise. Role-fit or "match" scores: weighted distance between the candidate's sten profile and an ideal job profile, often reported as 1–5 stars or "low/moderate/strong fit". This is inferred from general vendor practice.

## 6. Report types and their typical sections

### Takeaway
Vendors produce audience-specific reports from the same data: a recruiter/HR or administrator report (scores and norms, sometimes fit against a hiring threshold); a candidate feedback report (plain-language strengths and development areas); an interviewer report or interview guide (scale-based probing questions); a development report; and a team or "types" report.

### Cited Findings
- One vendor's portfolio includes an ability report for recruiters and a candidate-facing version, a personal feedback report, and an interviewer report "with suggestions for interview questions". — [Clevry: available personality reports](https://help.clevry.com/what-personality-reports-are-available); [Clevry: module 4 reports](https://help.clevry.com/module-4-clevrys-reports)
- Candidate feedback report: candidate-friendly personality description, up to 5 development areas, and career suggestions. — [Clevry reports](https://help.clevry.com/module-4-clevrys-reports)
- Interviewer report: shows responses per scale, provides strategic questions, and advises how to adapt for self-presentation or impression-management flags. — [Clevry interviewer report](https://www.clevry.com/en/resources/sample-reports/interviewer-report/); [Clevry: how to use the interviewer report](https://www.clevry.com/en/?p=10267)
- Recommended practice: form hypotheses from combined scale results, then test them in interview. — [Clevry: discussing results](https://help.clevry.com/discussing-results-with-candidates)
- A BPS-format test review of a major work-styles tool lists interview guide, development and "types" reports, used under a trained test user. — [Saville Wave BPS test review (slideshare copy)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/saville-wave-professional-styles-2014/64914571)
- One platform distinguishes a "selection report" (score against a hiring threshold) from a development report. — [Testlify report types](https://help.testlify.com/article/699-types-of-reports-on-testlify)

### Inferences

| Report | Audience | Typical sections |
|---|---|---|
| HR / administrator | Recruiter, hiring manager | Candidate and test metadata; norm group used; score table (raw, percentile, sten/T, band); overall/composite; role-fit indicator; response-style or consistency flags; completion time; confidentiality note |
| Candidate feedback | Candidate | Plain-language intro; what the test measures; band descriptors (no raw norms or percentiles in some vendors); strengths; development areas (≤5); tips; disclaimer |
| Interview guide | Interviewer | Profile summary; 3–8 flagged scales (very high or low); per scale a behavioural hypothesis, 2–3 probing questions, and positive and negative indicators to listen for; rating grid |
| Development | Employee, coach | Scale-by-scale narrative; strengths and overplayed strengths; development actions; reflection questions |
| Team | Manager | Aggregate distribution per scale; team "type" mix; gaps and overlaps; collaboration risks |

### Gaps
- BPS Psychological Testing Centre test-user guidance on report content (Level A/B, now Test User: Occupational Ability/Personality) was not retrieved directly.

## 7. Typical candidate-facing test structure

### Takeaway
The standard flow is: welcome and consent; instructions; untimed practice questions with feedback (typically 2–5); the timed section with a visible timer, a progress indicator and an on-screen calculator where relevant; then a completion screen. Personality questionnaires replace practice with example items and "there are no right or wrong answers" messaging.

### Cited Findings
- Publishers offer practice tasks before the real test. For example, one vendor publishes downloadable "practice tasks" for its verbal test. — [Aon practice tasks: verbal](https://assessment.aon.com/aon.assessment/media/files/factsheets/Practice-Tasks_verbal.pdf)
- Prep-site practice sets are typically about 10 questions with explanations. Tests are typically 9–20 questions in 10–20 minutes for verbal. — [jobcannon verbal practice](https://jobcannon.io/practice/verbal-reasoning); [AssessmentDay](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/verbal/Free/Verbal-Reasoning-Test1-Questions.pdf)
- Instructions should check for negative marking (rare). — [practiceaptitudetests tips](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/verbal-reasoning-tips-and-tricks/)

### Inferences
Suggested mock flow per test:
1. Intro screen: test name, duration, item count, equipment (calculator, pen and paper), quiet environment.
2. Instructions: item format, how to answer, timer behaviour, whether you can go back (CAT does not allow going back), negative marking (none).
3. Practice: 2–4 untimed example items with correct-answer feedback (ability); 2–3 example statements (personality); 1 example scenario (SJT).
4. Timed section: section timer or per-item timer; progress bar; auto-submit at timeout.
5. Completion screen: thank-you, next steps, whether feedback will be provided.

### Gaps
- No primary source fixed the typical number of practice items. The 2–4 count is inferred from common practice.
