# Psychometric Assessments in Recruitment: Southern Africa Compliance (South Africa, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Botswana)

Research date: 19 November 2026. Not legal advice. Items marked **[unverified this session]** come from background knowledge of the statutes and were not re-fetched; check them against the primary text before building rules on them. Research budget was limited (about 17 tool calls). Several primary sources (SAFLII, Webber Wentzel, Bowmans) returned 403 errors or timed out, so some points rest on secondary summaries.

## Q1. South Africa: EEA s8, HPCSA certification, test classification and who may administer

### Takeaway
The HPCSA certification requirement is no longer law. Section 8(d) of the EEA, added by the 2013 amendment, was declared not in operation by the High Court in 2017 (ATP-SA v President of RSA), and it was formally deleted from the Act with effect from **1 January 2025**. What remains binding is s8(a)–(c): a test must be scientifically shown to be valid and reliable, be applied fairly to all employees, and not be biased against any employee or group. Separately, under the Health Professions Act, tests the HPCSA has *classified* as psychological tests may be used only by HPCSA-registered psychology practitioners working within their scope. Classification governs who may use a test. It says nothing about the test's quality.

### Cited Findings
- Current s8 wording: "Psychological testing and other similar assessments of an employee are prohibited unless the test or assessment being used— (a) has been scientifically shown to be valid and reliable; (b) can be applied fairly to all employees; and (c) is not biased against any employee or group." — [EEA s8, acts.co.za](https://source.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act-1998/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php)
- Para (c) was amended by s4 of Act 47 of 2013. Para (d) (the HPCSA/authorised-body certification requirement) was deleted by s2 of the Employment Equity Amendment Act 4 of 2022 (Notice 3280, GG 48418, 14 April 2023), effective **1 January 2025** under Commencement Proclamation 231, GG 51684, 28 November 2024. — [acts.co.za s8](https://source.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act-1998/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php)
- Case: *Association of Test Publishers of South Africa v President of the RSA and Others* (89564/14) [2017] ZAGPPHC 144, reported at (2017) 38 ILJ 2253 (GP), decided 3 May 2017. The court declared Proclamation 50 (GG 37871) null and void and of no force and effect to the extent that it brought s8(d) into operation. ATP-SA had argued that requiring HPCSA certification was irrational. — [SAFLII](https://www1.saflii.org/za/cases/ZAGPPHC/2017/144.html) (403 on fetch; details from the search index); [Juta ILJ October 2017](https://juta.co.za/media/filestore/2017/09/ILJ_October.pdf); [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/court-cancels-new-job-test-provisos); [SA Labour News](https://salabournews.co.za/37376-court-cancels-proviso-on-hpcsa-certification-of-psychological-tests-for-jobs)
- The explanatory memorandum to the 2022 amendment gave the reason for deletion: the HPCSA "does not have the capacity or procedures to certify" these tests and assessments. — summarised in search results referencing [acts.co.za](https://source.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act-1998/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php) and [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=836433)
- One secondary source refers to a "2019 High Court ruling" finding the certification requirement irrational. This conflicts with the reported 2017 judgment date, and the 2017 date is the one supported by the SAFLII citation. No appeal outcome was found. — [Webber Wentzel update](https://www.webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/psychological-testing-and-other-similar-assessments-of-employees-an-update.aspx) (timed out on fetch)
- The HPCSA Professional Board for Psychology's Psychometrics Committee classifies tests as psychological or not and publishes a list of classified tests (Form 207, "List of Tests Classified as Being Psychological Tests", with reference numbers). The copy found is an old edition (file suffix 1011, so about 2010–11). — [UFS copy of Form 207](https://www.ufs.ac.za/docs/librariesprovider25/cpd-documents/cpd-psychological-tests-(form207)-1011-eng.pdf?sfvrsn=0)
- The HPCSA only classifies tests. It does not certify their quality. Tests on the classified list may only be used by appropriately qualified psychology professionals registered with the HPCSA. — search summary of [Webber Wentzel](https://www.webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/psychological-testing-and-other-similar-assessments-of-employees-an-update.aspx) and [PsySSA "Psychological Testing and Assessment"](https://www.psyssa.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Psychological-Testing-and-Assessment.pdf)
- The scope of practice for each psychology category (psychologist, registered counsellor, psychometrist) is in the Regulations Defining the Scope of the Profession of Psychology, GN R.993 of 2008, later amended (R704 of 2011). — [HPCSA GNR 993/2008](https://www.hpcsa.co.za/Uploads/professional_boards/psb/regulations/regulations_gnr993_2008.pdf); [R704 amendment](https://www.acts.co.za/health/r704_amendment_of_the_regulations.php); [gov.za GN 704](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/34581rg9582gon704.pdf)
- Psychometrists may administer, score and give a preliminary interpretation of psychological tests, but not projective personality techniques. An HPCSA FAQ says psychometrists and registered counsellors may not use certain tests such as projectives, personality and neuropsychological tests. — [Form 207 (UFS)](https://www.ufs.ac.za/docs/librariesprovider25/cpd-documents/cpd-psychological-tests-(form207)-1011-eng.pdf?sfvrsn=0); [HPCSA psychology FAQ (UFS copy)](https://www.ufs.ac.za/docs/librariesprovider25/cpd-documents/cpd-psychology-frequent-asked-questions-1015-eng.pdf)
- The psychometry framework (Form 94) sets the training, registration and scope of psychometrists, including the independent-practice and supervised categories. — [Form 94, Stellenbosch copy](https://www.sun.ac.za/english/faculty/economy/industrial-psychology/Documents/Form_94_Psychometry_Framework_for_Education%20_Training_Registration_and_Scope_for_Psychometry.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Product rule (SA):** Do not show "HPCSA-certified" as a legal requirement or a compliance badge. Since 1 January 2025 there is no certification requirement in the statute, and the HPCSA never ran a certification process. Instead, record for each test: (1) whether it appears on the HPCSA classified list (Form 207), and if so its reference number; (2) the evidence for validity and reliability, ideally SA-normed (technical manual, norm group, date); (3) bias and differential item functioning evidence for SA groups.
- **Gating:** If a test is HPCSA-classified, the platform should require a named HPCSA-registered practitioner (registration number, category such as Psychologist (Industrial), Psychometrist or Registered Counsellor) to be assigned before sending. Interpretive reports for classified tests should be released only to, or through, that practitioner. Personality tests should be blocked from psychometrist or registered counsellor accounts unless a psychologist supervises (the supervision rules were not confirmed this session; see Gaps).
- **Scope of s8:** s8 refers to an "employee". Under EEA s9 **[unverified this session]**, "employee" includes an applicant for employment for the purposes of the anti-discrimination chapter. Treat candidates as covered.
- **Burden of proof:** Under EEA s11 **[unverified this session]**, once discrimination is alleged on a listed ground, the employer must prove it is rational and not unfair, or otherwise justifiable. The platform should therefore keep evidence of validity and fairness, and the actual test version used, per assessment event.
- **Tests outside the definition:** Skills or work-sample tests (typing, coding, Excel) are "other similar assessments" if used to make employment decisions. They still need s8(a)–(c) evidence but are usually not HPCSA-classified, so no practitioner gating is needed. The product should use separate categories such as "Psychological test (HPCSA-classified)", "Psychometric/other similar assessment (not classified)" and "Skills/knowledge test".
- **Language:** SA has 12 official languages (South African Sign Language was added by the Constitution Eighteenth Amendment Act, 2023) **[unverified this session]**. If a test is delivered in a candidate's second language without evidence of measurement equivalence, that could undermine s8(b)–(c). Record the test language, the candidate's home or preferred language, and whether norms exist for that language.

### Gaps
- The current (2024–2026) HPCSA Form 207 classified list was not retrieved. Get it from hpcsa.co.za (Professional Board for Psychology > Forms).
- Whether an appeal was lodged against the 2017 ATP-SA judgment, and whether the Webber Wentzel "2019" reference is an error or a separate decision, is unresolved.
- No SIOPSA or ATP-SA guidance documents (for example the SIOPSA Code of Practice for Psychological and Other Similar Assessment in the Workplace) were retrieved.
- The current HPCSA supervision rules for psychometrists (supervised vs independent practice) and whether supervision lets them use personality tests were not confirmed.

## Q2. South Africa: POPIA

### Takeaway
Test results are personal information. Responses that disclose health, disability or race are special personal information. Results that are scored and used for automated shortlisting are restricted by s71, which bars decisions based solely on automated processing that profile work performance or reliability unless an exception applies and the candidate can make representations. The employer is the responsible party. The software vendor is an operator.

### Cited Findings
- Under s71, a data subject may not be subjected to a decision with legal consequences or a substantial effect that is based solely on automated processing intended to profile them, including evaluation of "performance at work", reliability, health, personal preferences or conduct. — [Werksmans, "When machines make decisions"](https://werksmans.com/when-machines-make-decisions-understanding-the-impact-of-the-protection-of-personal-information-act-2013-popia/)
- Exceptions under s71(2): the decision is taken in connection with a contract and either the data subject's request was met or appropriate measures protect their legitimate interests, or it is governed by a law or code of conduct that specifies such measures. Appropriate measures must give the person an opportunity to make representations and must provide enough information about the underlying logic of the automated processing. — [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/when-machines-make-decisions-understanding-the-impact-of-the-protection-of-personal-information-act-2013-popia/)
- The employer remains the responsible party where a recruitment agency or AI provider processes the data. s18 notification duties apply to candidates. — [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/when-machines-make-decisions-understanding-the-impact-of-the-protection-of-personal-information-act-2013-popia/)
- No Information Regulator guidance note specific to recruitment or psychometrics was found. — search of the Regulator and law-firm sources, [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/when-machines-make-decisions-understanding-the-impact-of-the-protection-of-personal-information-act-2013-popia/)
- POPIA Act text (official) — [Information Regulator / DoJ POPIA PDF](https://www.justice.gov.za/inforeg/docs/InfoRegSA-POPIA-act2013-004.pdf) **[not fetched this session]**

### Inferences
These map statute sections to product rules from background knowledge of POPIA. **[unverified this session; check against the Act]**
- **Lawful basis (s11):** Recruitment testing usually relies on steps taken to conclude a contract at the data subject's request, or on legitimate interest. Consent is optional but can be withdrawn. The product should record which basis the employer has chosen.
- **Special personal information (ss26–33):** This includes race, health (including mental health) and biometrics. Psychometric items or accommodation requests that reveal health or disability need a s27 ground, typically explicit consent or a right or obligation in law (EEA reasonable accommodation). Store accommodation and disability data separately from scores, with restricted access. Note that race data collected for EEA reporting is permitted under the EEA, but it must not feed scoring.
- **Notification (s18):** Before testing, tell the candidate the responsible party's identity and contact details, the purpose, whether the information is voluntary or mandatory and the consequences of not supplying it, the recipients, any cross-border transfer, and their rights (access, correction, objection, complaint to the Information Regulator).
- **Retention (s14):** Keep data no longer than needed for the purpose. In practice, set a configurable retention period for unsuccessful candidates. Six to twelve months is common practice, not a statutory figure; it lets the employer defend a CCMA or Labour Court referral. Delete or de-identify afterwards. The EEA's own record-keeping rules may justify longer retention for employment-equity records.
- **Access (s23):** Candidates can request their results, though raw item data for classified tests may be withheld to protect test security (an ethical rule, not a POPIA exemption). Provide practitioner-mediated feedback.
- **Cross-border (s72):** If the vendor hosts results outside SA, disclose this and rely on adequacy, binding corporate rules or agreements, consent, or contract necessity.
- **Operators (ss20–21):** A written operator agreement with security measures, and breach notification to the Regulator and data subjects as soon as reasonably possible (s22).
- **s71 product enforcement:** No auto-reject based solely on a test score. Require a human review step (a named reviewer and timestamp) before any adverse status change. Give candidates a "request human review / make representations" link and a plain-language explanation of how scores feed decisions.

### Gaps
- No Information Regulator enforcement notices or guidance specific to recruitment were found.
- The POPIA section numbers above should be confirmed against the official text.

## Q3. Zimbabwe: CDPA [Chapter 12:07], SI 155 of 2024, Labour Act, psychology regulation

### Takeaway
Zimbabwe has the strictest administrative regime of the four. Any controller processing personal data of 50 or more people must hold a POTRAZ data controller licence and appoint a DPO. Sensitive data generally needs written consent. Cross-border transfers require adequacy and POTRAZ notification. Candidates have a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions unless they consent or a law authorises it. Expert commentary says only registered psychologists should administer and interpret psychometric tests, but no statutory test-classification scheme like the HPCSA list was found.

### Cited Findings
- CDPA Act No. 5 of 2021, gazetted on 3 December 2021 as the Data Protection Act and in force as the Cyber and Data Protection Act from 11 March 2022. POTRAZ is the Data Protection Authority (s5). — [Veritas CDPA PDF](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf); [ZimLII](https://zimlii.org/akn/zw/act/2021/5/eng@2022-03-11/source); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/understanding-si-155-of-2024-new-regulations-on-data-protection-licensing-and-data-protection-officers-in-zimbabwe)
- Sensitive data (s11) requires written consent, which can be withdrawn at any time (secondary summary). — [Michalsons CDPA overview](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/zimbabwes-cyber-and-data-protection-act-overview/78795); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?c=ZW&c2=NG&t=law)
- s25: data subjects have a right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produce legal effects. This does not apply where the data subject consented or a law provides for it. — [Veritas CDPA PDF](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf) (via search excerpt)
- ss28–29: transfer outside Zimbabwe is allowed only if the recipient country ensures adequate protection, and the controller must notify the Authority. The DPA may prescribe countries to which transfer is not permitted (s28(3)). s29 covers transfers to non-adequate countries. — [UWC thesis on Zimbabwe cross-border transfer](https://uwcscholar.uwc.ac.za/items/f096843a-947f-400c-8790-fc0c42b455f2); [Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/zimbabwes-cyber-and-data-protection-act-overview/78795)
- SI 155 of 2024, the Cyber and Data Protection (Licensing of Data Controllers and Appointment of Data Protection Officers) Regulations, took effect on 13 September 2024. Controllers processing data of 50 or more data subjects must obtain a POTRAZ licence. Four licence tiers are set by number of data subjects. — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/understanding-si-155-of-2024-new-regulations-on-data-protection-licensing-and-data-protection-officers-in-zimbabwe); [The Standard](https://thestandard.co.zw/standard-people/article/200033237/statutory-instrument-155-of-2024-a-new-era-for-data-protection-in-zimbabwe)
- DPO: must be appointed and notified to POTRAZ within 90 days. The DPO needs relevant qualifications, a DPA-approved certification course and independence. Breaches go to POTRAZ within 24 hours, and to data subjects within 72 hours if high risk. Penalties run up to level 11 fines, up to 7 years' imprisonment, or both. — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/understanding-si-155-of-2024-new-regulations-on-data-protection-licensing-and-data-protection-officers-in-zimbabwe)
- Deadlines conflict: sources give the licence or DPO deadline as 3 months, 6 months (13 March 2025) or 12 March 2025. — [DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/zimbabwe/insights/2024/A-Quick-Start-Guide-to-Zimbabwes-Data-Protection-Regulations); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/data-protection/1626192/data-protection-obligations-in-zimbabwe-is-your-organisation-aligned); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/understanding-si-155-of-2024-new-regulations-on-data-protection-licensing-and-data-protection-officers-in-zimbabwe)
- Only POTRAZ-licensed persons may process biometric data. — [search summary of Afriwise/Business Times](https://businesstimes.co.zw/understanding-the-purpose-scope-of-the-cyber-and-data-protection-licensing-regulations-in-zimbabwe/)
- Licence fees reportedly range from US$50 to US$2,500 a year. — [Connecting Africa](https://connectingafrica.com/regulation/concerns-grow-over-zimbabwean-licensing-fees-for-whatsapp-group-admins); [CITE](https://cite.org.zw/potraz-data-licence-fees-slammed-as-costly-burden-on-businesses-and-consumers/)
- POTRAZ Regulatory Notice 2 of 2026 announced mandatory compliance inspections and assessments of data controllers from 1 September 2026. — reported in search results ([Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/potraz-urges-compliance-with-data-protection-laws/)); the primary notice was not viewed
- Labour Act s5 protects employees **and prospective employees** from discrimination (grounds start with race and tribe and include political opinion, colour, creed, gender, pregnancy, HIV/AIDS status and disability, read with Constitution s56(3)). It gives a damages claim and possible criminal liability (s5(3)). — [Chambers Employment 2025, Zimbabwe](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/employment-2025/813); [WageIndicator Zimbabwe](https://wageindicator.org/en-zw/ai/work-in-zimbabwe/labour-law/fair-treatment/); [Labour Act (ILO copy)](https://www.ilo.org/dyn/travail/docs/2287/Labour%20Act%20-%20Re-issued%201%20February%202006.pdf)
- The Labour Amendment Act 2023 replaced s5(2a) with an equal-pay duty. Its commencement was not confirmed. — [Chambers](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/employment-2025/813)
- Expert commentary: companies should make sure that people who administer and interpret psychometric tests are psychologists registered with the Allied Health Practitioners Council of Zimbabwe (AHPCZ). — [The Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/engage-registered-psychologists-for-pre-employment-tests-expert/)
- AHPCZ registers psychologists (a postgraduate psychology qualification or supervised internship is required, with an annual practising certificate). It deregistered more than 70 practitioners in 2024. — [TherapyRoute](https://www.therapyroute.com/article/government-regulatory-bodies-for-allied-mental-health-professions-by-therapyroute) (low-authority source); [ZBC](https://www.zbcnews.co.zw/?p=34667)

### Inferences
- **Product (ZW):** Before allowing tests to be sent to Zimbabwean candidates, require the employer to enter its POTRAZ licence number and DPO contact. Treat written (recorded, explicit) consent as the default for any test touching health or personality, or anything that could count as sensitive. Because s25 allows automated decisions on consent, show candidates an explicit opt-in for automated scoring and provide a human-review fallback. If data is hosted outside Zimbabwe (for example in SA or the EU), disclose it and log the employer's notification to POTRAZ. Any facial or voice proctoring is biometric processing and needs POTRAZ licensing, so default it to off for ZW.
- Practitioner gating should be "recommended" rather than "enforced" for ZW: assign an AHPCZ-registered psychologist for clinical or personality instruments.

### Gaps
- No exact SI 155 tier thresholds or fees from the gazetted text. The compliance deadline is conflicting.
- Whether Zimbabwe statute restricts psychological test use to registered psychologists (as SA does) was not confirmed. The Herald piece is opinion.
- The full s5 Labour Act grounds list and the Labour Amendment Act 2023 commencement were not confirmed.

## Q4. Zambia: Data Protection Act No. 3 of 2021, Employment Code Act 2019, HPCZ

### Takeaway
Zambia requires controllers and processors to register with the Data Protection Commissioner and protects against solely automated decisions with significant effects. It also imposes localisation and consent limits on sensitive personal data, which matters for hosting test results that contain health or psychological data. The Employment Code Act s5 bars direct and indirect discrimination against prospective employees, including at recruitment. Psychologists are regulated under health professions legislation, and the 2009 Act was replaced by the Health Professions Act 2024.

### Cited Findings
- DPA No. 3 of 2021 entered into force on 1 April 2021 (Commencement Orders). — [ZambiaLII DPA 2021](https://zambialii.org/akn/zm/act/2021/3/eng@2021-03-24); [DataProtection.Africa Zambia](https://dataprotection.africa/zambia/)
- Controllers and processors must register with the Data Protection Commissioner (s20(1) per Bowmans). Data Protection (Registration and Licensing) Regulations categorise controllers. — [Bowmans, Zambia employers' obligations](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/zambia-employers-obligations-under-the-data-protection-act/) (403 on fetch; search summary); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/zambia-employers-obligations-under-the-data-protection-act)
- Sensitive personal data may not be transferred outside Zambia without consent, and must generally be stored within Zambia unless an exemption applies. — [DataProtection.Africa](https://dataprotection.africa/zambia/); [Bowmans (search summary)](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/zambia-employers-obligations-under-the-data-protection-act/)
- Data subjects have a right to object to decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, with legal or similarly significant effects. — [DataProtection.Africa](https://dataprotection.africa/zambia/); [CaseGuard](https://caseguard.com/?p=23197)
- Breach-notification timing conflicts: one source says 24 hours, another says there is no specific timeline. — search results ([DataProtection.Africa](https://dataprotection.africa/zambia/); [Paradigm Initiative digest](https://paradigmhq.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Digital-Policy-Digest-No-1-of-2023-v3.pdf))
- Employment Code Act 2019, s5 "Non-discrimination at undertaking", bars direct and indirect discrimination against employees and prospective employees, including in recruitment. It is unlawful to ask about pregnancy during recruitment. The Act came into effect in May 2020. — [WageIndicator Zambia](https://wageindicator.org/en-zm/work-in-zambia/labour-law/fair-treatment); [Employment Code Act (FAOLEX)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/zam224519.pdf); [Lusaka Times](https://www.lusakatimes.com/2020/05/14/a-new-act-to-regulate-the-employment-of-persons-in-zambia-comes-into-effect)
- Health Professions Act 24 of 2009 established the HPCZ, and practitioners may not practise unless registered (s6(1)). Zambian mental health policy expects clinical psychologists to be licensed under the HPA. The 2009 Act was repealed on 26 December 2024 by the Health Professions Act 2024 (Act 17 of 2024). — [ZambiaLII HPA 2009](https://zambialii.org/akn/zm/act/2009/24/eng@2021-03-24); [Health Professions Bill NAB 11 of 2024](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/bills/N.A.B%2011%20of%202024%2C%20The%20Health%20Professions%20Bill.pdf); [Policy Vault mental health policy](https://www.policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/ZMB540.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Product (ZM):** Treat psychometric results with any health or clinical element as sensitive personal data. Default to in-country storage, or obtain explicit consent for offshore hosting and record it. Require the employer's Data Protection Commissioner registration number. Use the same human-review and objection mechanism as SA s71.
- Industrial or organisational psychologists may not clearly fall under HPCZ registration (which centres on clinical practice). Use "recommend a registered psychologist" rather than enforce gating.

### Gaps
- The exact section numbers for the sensitive-data, localisation and automated-decision provisions were not confirmed from statute text.
- Whether HPCZ regulates non-clinical (industrial or organisational) psychologists or psychometric test use under the 2024 Act was not found.
- The full list of s5 Employment Code grounds was not retrieved.

## Q5. Botswana: Data Protection Act 2024, employment law, psychology regulation

### Takeaway
The Data Protection Act 2024 (Act 18 of 2024) repealed the 2018 Act, which never effectively commenced. The Information and Data Protection Commission (IDPC) continues as regulator. Employment law changed in 2026: the Employment and Labour Relations Act 2025 consolidates the Employment Act and related statutes and reportedly came into force on 1 September 2026, with discrimination protections covering recruitment. The Persons with Disability Act 2024 adds disability non-discrimination in employment. Psychologists are registered by the Botswana Health Professions Council (BHPC). No test-classification scheme was found.

### Cited Findings
- The DPA 2024 repealed the DPA 2018, whose effective date was repeatedly deferred by Ministerial Orders. The IDPC continues, with new Data Protection and Access to Information divisions and added search and seizure powers. — [CIPIT Strathmore](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/understanding-botswanas-2018-and-2024-data-protection-acts/); [MISA](https://misa.org/blog/new-botswana-data-protection-act-progressive-step-to-a-more-secure-digital-environment/); [Botswana DP Bill 2024 (Michalsons)](https://www.michalsons.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Botswana-Data-Protection-Bill-2024.pdf)
- Commencement dates conflict: 29 October 2024 or 14 January 2025, with assent reported on 19 August 2024. — [CIPIT](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/understanding-botswanas-2018-and-2024-data-protection-acts/); [Techhive Advisory](https://www.techhiveadvisory.africa/insights/understanding-botswanas-new-data-protection-act-key-updates-and-changes); [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/further-into-africabotswana-enacts-a-new-data-protection-act-does-this-spell-a-new-dawn/page/208/)
- Statute portal. — [Botswana Laws, Data Protection](https://botswanalaws.com/consolidated-statutes/principle-legislation/data-protection); [DLA Piper Botswana](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=BW&t=law)
- The Employment and Labour Relations Act 2025 consolidates the Employment Act, the Trade Unions and Employers' Organisations Act and the Trade Disputes Act, and is reported in force from 1 September 2026. It protects against discrimination including on gender, HIV status and disability, and reportedly extends to recruitment. — [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/botswana-advances-decent-work-landmark-labour-reform); [Daily News Botswana](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/93207); [DLA Piper proposed bill](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/proposed-new-employment-and-labour-relations-bill)
- The Persons with Disability Act 2024 (Act 6 of 2024) prohibits disability discrimination in employment and by employment agencies. — [Act 6 of 2024 PDF](https://covid19.gov.bw/sites/default/files/2025-03/Act%20No%206%20of%202024%20PERSONS%20WITH%20DISABILITY%20%281%29.pdf); [Botswana Laws](https://testelaws.botswanalaws.com/?p=36587)
- The Botswana Health Professions Act 17 of 2001 (Cap 61:02) establishes the BHPC, which registers practitioners. ## Q6. Practical delivery: multilingual, low-bandwidth, proctoring, accessibility

### Takeaway
Little authoritative source material was gathered this session. The legal hooks are fairness under EEA s8(b)–(c), the reasonable-accommodation duties in disability law, and data minimisation and sensitive-data rules for proctoring (biometrics). Design recommendations below are inferences.

### Cited Findings
- Biometric processing in Zimbabwe is restricted to POTRAZ-licensed persons. — [Business Times ZW](https://businesstimes.co.zw/understanding-the-purpose-scope-of-the-cyber-and-data-protection-licensing-regulations-in-zimbabwe/)
- Botswana Persons with Disability Act 2024 prohibits employment discrimination against persons with disabilities. — [Act 6 of 2024](https://covid19.gov.bw/sites/default/files/2025-03/Act%20No%206%20of%202024%20PERSONS%20WITH%20DISABILITY%20%281%29.pdf)
- EEA s8(b): a test must be capable of being "applied fairly to all employees". — [acts.co.za](https://source.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act-1998/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php)

### Inferences
- **Multilingual:** Offer only languages for which the publisher has validated or normed versions. Log the language used. Let candidates choose the language before starting and record that choice. Do not machine-translate psychometric items.
- **Low bandwidth and mobile:** Preload items, keep pages light, avoid video by default, and save progress after each answer. Use server-authoritative timers that pause on disconnect, with a grace period and audit log, so connectivity drops are not penalised (an unfair-application risk under s8(b)). Check that the test is device-agnostic, or record device type to monitor adverse impact. Zero-rated data deals with MNOs are a commercial option, not a legal requirement.
- **Proctoring:** Webcam or face recognition is biometric or special data. In SA it needs a s27 ground; in ZW, a POTRAZ licence. Default to non-biometric integrity measures (item randomisation, time stamps, browser focus events) with disclosure. If video proctoring is used, give a clear notice, a short retention period and human review of any flags (no auto-fail; POPIA s71 / CDPA s25 / Zambia DPA).
- **Accommodations:** Ask about accommodation needs before the test (extra time, screen reader compatibility, large print, sign-language instructions). Store the request in a restricted field that is separate from scores and route it to the practitioner. Log that accommodations were granted. The SA EEA Code of Good Practice on Employment of Persons with Disabilities **[unverified this session]** requires reasonable accommodation in assessments.
- **Adverse impact:** Provide employer dashboards with selection ratios by EEA group (the four-fifths rule is a common heuristic, not SA law) using EEA-collected demographics kept separate from scoring.

### Gaps
- No SA Journal of Industrial Psychology or SIOPSA sources on mobile or remote testing equivalence, or on remote proctoring, were retrieved this session.
- No data-cost or zero-rating figures were collected.

## Q7. Candidate consent and information notice contents, plus compliance table

### Takeaway
One pre-test notice and consent screen, localised per country, can meet all four regimes. It should identify the employer as controller and the vendor as processor, explain the purpose, the test type and whether a psychologist is involved, list who sees results, state hosting location and cross-border transfer, give the retention period, explain how scores feed decisions with a right to human review, offer accommodations and feedback, and link to the regulator complaint route. Zimbabwe requires written, explicit consent for sensitive data and for automated decisions.

### Cited Findings
- POPIA s71 safeguards require an opportunity for representations and information about the logic of the processing. — [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/when-machines-make-decisions-understanding-the-impact-of-the-protection-of-personal-information-act-2013-popia/)
- Zimbabwe CDPA s25 exception relies on consent. Sensitive data needs written consent. — [Veritas CDPA](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf); [Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/zimbabwes-cyber-and-data-protection-act-overview/78795)
- Zambia: consent is required to transfer sensitive data abroad. — [DataProtection.Africa](https://dataprotection.africa/zambia/)

### Inferences: minimum notice contents
1. Controller identity and contact details (employer) and DPO or information officer. Operator or processor (vendor).
2. Purpose: assessment for role X, plus any secondary use (norming or research, de-identified, with a separate opt-in).
3. Assessment details: name, publisher, what it measures, duration, language, whether it is an HPCSA-classified psychological test, and the supervising practitioner's name and registration number.
4. Lawful basis, and whether participation is voluntary, with the consequence of declining (for example, the application may not proceed) and alternative arrangements.
5. Who sees results: hiring manager (summary level), the practitioner (full report), and any third parties.
6. Storage location and cross-border transfer (country), with safeguards.
7. Retention period and deletion date.
8. Automated decision-making: whether scores are used to rank or filter, the logic in plain language, the existence of human review, and how to request review or make representations.
9. Rights: access, correction, objection, deletion, feedback on results (practitioner-delivered for psychological tests), and complaint to the regulator (SA Information Regulator; ZW POTRAZ; ZM Data Protection Commissioner; BW IDPC).
10. Accommodations: how to request them confidentially before starting.
11. Proctoring: what is monitored, whether it is biometric, and how long it is retained.
12. Consent capture: separate checkboxes for (a) the assessment, (b) special or sensitive data, (c) automated scoring (needed in ZW), (d) cross-border transfer (needed in ZM for sensitive data), and (e) research reuse. Timestamp, version and language of the notice are logged.
